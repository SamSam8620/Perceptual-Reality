# Storyboard — Detmerode to Porschestrasse
## The Same Route. Two Realities.

---

## The Two Users

**Leila** — 34. Has lived in Detmerode for four years. Drives to the city center, to the supermarket, to work. Has never walked to Porschestrasse. Knows it exists. Assumes it takes too long. On this day, her car is in the garage.

**Erik** — 28. Cycles from Detmerode to the center three times a week. Knows the route by feel. Doesn't think about it. Uses it.

The installations are the same for both. What changes is what each person brings to them.

---

---

## Scene 1 — Leaving the Estate
### Setting: Detmerode housing estate. Morning. Overcast.

The estate opens up in every direction. Wide spaces between towers. Grass. A few parked bikes. The towers — locals call them Don Camillo and Peppone, after the Italian film characters — rise above the roofline. The Stephanuskirche sits low and angular nearby, Alvar Aalto's truncated wedge of concrete, quiet at this hour.

There are paths everywhere. None of them announce themselves as the right one.

---

**LEILA**

She steps outside and immediately feels the exposure. The open space between blocks is bigger than she remembered. She checks her phone — puts it away. She's not using her phone today.

At the first path junction, a low post glows at waist height. Not bright — just present. An arrow faces north. Below it:

> *Porschestrasse — 3.8 km*
> *247 people walked this route today*

She looks at the number. 247. She didn't expect that. She assumed she'd be alone in this.

She follows the arrow.

At the next junction, another post. Same quiet glow. The arrow continues north. She doesn't have to think. The estate, which always felt like a maze of equivalent paths, suddenly has a direction.

She passes the Stephanuskirche. She has walked past it before but never looked. A small panel beside the path — embedded in a low wall, not a screen, etched and backlit:

> *Designed by Alvar Aalto, 1963–1968.*
> *One of three buildings he made in Wolfsburg —*
> *more than in any city outside Finland.*
> *The ceiling holds nineteen floating discs.*

She glances up at the building. She had no idea.

**Emotional state:** Uncertain → oriented. The route is starting to feel like something that was designed for her.

---

**ERIK**

He cycles through the estate without thinking about the posts. He knows the route. But as he passes the Stephanuskirche panel — the one he's ridden past fifty times — he actually reads it today because he's stopped to adjust his bag.

*Nineteen floating discs.* He's never been inside.

He makes a mental note. Continues north.

**Emotional state:** Routine → a small puncture of curiosity.

---

---

## Scene 2 — The Bridge
### Setting: Approach to the Zusammenrücken Bridge. The sound of traffic on Braunschweiger Straße below.

The bridge rises ahead — 90 metres of concrete and steel crossing above one of the city's main arterial roads. It opened in 2021. Its name, *Zusammenrücken*, means "coming closer together." Most people who use it don't know that.

---

**LEILA**

She hears the traffic before she sees the road. The bridge is elevated — she'll walk above all of it.

As she steps onto the bridge approach, something shifts. The paving lights along the bridge edge activate quietly ahead of her — not dramatically, just enough to mark the path forward. A low tone sounds, barely audible above the traffic. Not a notification. More like a breath.

At the near end of the bridge, a marker is set into the railing:

> *Zusammenrücken — "coming closer together"*
> *Detmerode to Westhagen, 2021*
>
> *You are 25 minutes from Porschestrasse.*

She stops. Reads it. *25 minutes.* Less than she thought.

She walks across. Below her, cars move fast in both directions. She is above them, moving slowly, and she doesn't feel like she's losing. She feels like she's on a different system entirely.

At the far end: the forest begins.

**Emotional state:** The first real threshold. She is no longer in the estate. She is in the route.

---

**ERIK**

He crosses the bridge fast, standing up on the pedals. The lights are barely noticeable at his speed. But the name — *Zusammenrücken* — catches him. He knew the bridge. He didn't know the name.

He rolls into the forest.

**Emotional state:** Moving fast, but accumulating small things.

---

---

## Scene 3 — Rothehofer Forst
### Setting: Protected landscape conservation corridor. Trees on both sides. The city disappears.

This is the strangest part of the route. You cross a bridge over a motorway and within thirty seconds you are in a forest. The city planned it this way — a green buffer between districts, a deliberate separation. For decades it kept Detmerode and Westhagen apart. The bridge is what finally joined them.

The path is narrow. In winter, it is dark by four in the afternoon. In any season, it is quiet.

---

**LEILA**

The forest closes around her. She wasn't expecting this.

The ground-level lighting along the path activates 40 metres ahead of her — low, warm, just bright enough to read the surface — and fades gently 40 metres behind. She is always at the centre of a moving pool of light. The forest isn't lit. She is lit within it.

She can hear her own footsteps.

Halfway through the corridor — she knows it's halfway because a marker says so, set low into a timber post:

> *Halfway.*
> *You have walked 1.9 km.*
> *19 minutes to Porschestrasse.*

And then, quietly, from a small directional speaker in the post, a voice — calm, unhurried:

> *"This forest was planned as a boundary.*
> *In the 1960s, Wolfsburg's districts were built to be self-contained.*
> *Detmerode on one side. Westhagen on the other.*
> *The bridge you just crossed didn't exist until 2021.*
> *You are walking a route that wasn't possible three years ago."*

She stands still for a moment.

She is not tired. She has been walking for twenty minutes and she is not tired. She has covered nearly half the distance to the city center. She didn't know she could do this.

**Emotional state:** Unexpectedly calm. The forest feels safe. The audio makes her feel part of something — the history of the city, the slow project of connection.

---

**ERIK**

He cycles through in under two minutes, the lighting activating and fading fast at his pace. The audio at the halfway post is too brief to catch — just a fragment of a voice. He makes a note to walk this section once.

The forest floor is damp. Good grip. He exits into Westhagen.

**Emotional state:** Enjoying the ride. Something is accumulating.

---

---

## Scene 4 — Westhagen
### Setting: Emerging from the forest into Westhagen. Similar housing character to Detmerode — towers, open space — but mid-renewal. Some scaffolding. New paving in places.

Westhagen is in transition. The city has designated it a Sanierungsgebiet — a renewal zone. Money is coming in. Buildings are being refaced. It looks like a district finding a new version of itself.

---

**LEILA**

She emerges from the trees onto a wider path. The trees are gone and the sky opens up again. For a moment she loses the direction — the forest had a single axis, but here there are choices again.

A wayfinding post at the forest exit. Arrow: north. Below:

> *Westhagen*
> *15 minutes to Porschestrasse*
> *612 people passed this point today*

612. The number is higher than before. She is joining a flow, even if she can't see it. Other people — hundreds of them — walked exactly this path today.

She walks through Westhagen. The housing looks similar to Detmerode but different in feeling — more active, more scaffolding, more change in the air. A panel on a low wall:

> *Westhagen was built the same decade as Detmerode.*
> *Two districts, the same blueprint, the same distance from the center.*
> *Both are changing. So is the route between them.*

She reads it and keeps walking. She is 15 minutes from Porschestrasse.

She is not counting down anxiously. She is just walking.

**Emotional state:** Settled into the rhythm. The city is readable. She belongs in it on foot.

---

**ERIK**

He rolls through Westhagen fast, picking up the installations at pace — the footfall counter (612), the directional post. He nods at the number instinctively. He's one of them.

**Emotional state:** Part of the flow.

---

---

## Scene 5 — City Center Approach & Arrival
### Setting: The density increases. More people. Wider streets. The canal somewhere nearby. The scale of the city center beginning to assert itself.

The route shifts from corridor to city. The fabric tightens. Shopfronts, offices, more cyclists. The installations become more frequent — closer together, brighter, more social. The system knows you're almost there.

---

**LEILA**

She notices the posts are closer now. Every 200 metres, then every 100. The numbers count down:

> *10 minutes*
> *7 minutes*
> *4 minutes*

She passes under a canopy of responsive lights — she doesn't know it's reacting to her, but the lights along the final approach corridor are denser and brighter as pedestrian traffic increases. The path feels inhabited. She can see other people now — a man on a bike, two women talking, a child running ahead of its parent.

At the final post before Porschestrasse, an aggregate display:

> *Today, 1,847 people walked or cycled from the districts to the center.*
> *You are one of them.*

She steps onto Porschestrasse.

She has been walking for 46 minutes. She thought it would take over an hour and feel like a punishment. It felt like a route she'd want to use again.

She stands at the start of the pedestrian street. The cars are gone. The city center opens in front of her. She arrived here on foot, and the city registered it.

**Emotional state:** Arrival. Quiet satisfaction. The route gave her something — not just the destination but a different understanding of where she lives.

---

**ERIK**

He locks his bike at Porschestrasse. 18 minutes door to door. He's thinking about the Stephanuskirche — nineteen floating discs — and whether he'll walk back through the forest later.

He might.

**Emotional state:** The route he knew became slightly larger. That's enough.

---

---

## The Design Argument

Leila didn't need encouragement. She needed *information* — that 247 people had already done this today, that the route was 25 minutes from the bridge, that there was a forest halfway through, that it was all possible.

Erik didn't need wayfinding. He needed small punctures of curiosity — details about a place he thought he already knew.

The same installations served both. Neither carried a device. Neither was tracked. Neither gave up any data.

The route didn't change. The reality of walking it did.
